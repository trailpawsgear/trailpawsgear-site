These two trackers get shortlisted together constantly, and then people discover the comparison isn't really about GPS at all. Both find your dog. Both need a subscription to work. The decision comes down to three things almost nobody weighs properly before buying: how often you're willing to charge the thing, whether your trails have cell coverage, and whether you want to keep the collar your dog already wears.

Here's how the Fi Series 3+ and the Tractive GPS Dog XL actually differ on those three points.

The short version

Get the Fi Series 3+ if your dog is off-leash regularly and you want to stop thinking about charging. Get the Tractive GPS Dog XL if you want the lowest-cost entry into real live GPS, your dog is big or strong, and you'd rather clip a tracker onto the collar you already own than replace it.

Battery life: the difference is bigger than the numbers suggest

On paper this looks like eight-to-ten weeks against two-to-four. In practice the gap matters more than that ratio, because of what happens at the end of the cycle.

A tracker that lasts two months is something you charge when you remember, roughly when the seasons change. A tracker that lasts two to three weeks is something that lands in your routine, and routines fail. The failure mode for a dog tracker is not "the battery is low", it's "the battery was low on the one afternoon the dog went through the fence". That is the entire argument for the Fi.

Two caveats keep this from being a rout. The Tractive's power-saving zone mode genuinely stretches it well past two weeks when the dog is mostly at home, so if your off-leash days are occasional rather than daily, the real-world gap narrows. And continuous live tracking, the mode you actually want during a search, drains the Tractive in hours rather than days. That's fine for an active search and useless as a background setting.

Coverage: this is where the Tractive can simply fail

Both units are cellular. Neither is satellite. If there's no signal, there's no position, and this is the single most underrated point in the whole comparison.

The Tractive works across multiple carriers, which helps in patchy areas because it isn't tied to one network's map. The Fi runs on AT&T's LTE-M network, which is built for low-power devices and reaches into places normal phone coverage doesn't, but it's still one carrier.

The honest conclusion is uncomfortable for both: on genuinely remote trail, in a canyon, in dense timber far from a road, either tracker can go quiet. If that describes where you actually hike, no cellular tracker solves your problem, and you should be thinking about a satellite messenger for yourself and a bell or a bright vest for the dog. Multi-carrier gives the Tractive an edge in mixed suburban and rural coverage. Neither wins in a dead zone.

Attachment: the detail people regret

The Fi replaces the buckle on the collar. That makes it part of the collar rather than an accessory dangling off it, which is more secure and less likely to be torn away in brush, but it also means you are committing to Fi's collar system rather than the collar your dog already wears and you already trust.

The Tractive clips onto whatever collar you own. That's genuinely convenient, and it's also the weaker mechanical link. A clip-on module on a dog that crashes through undergrowth is a thing that can be found on the trail behind you.

If your dog wears a specific collar for a reason, for medical ID, for a particular width, for a martingale fit, the Tractive keeps that setup intact and the Fi doesn't.

The cost that matters is the one you keep paying

Both require an active subscription. Neither is a one-time purchase, and neither device functions at all without a plan, which means the hardware price is close to irrelevant when you look past the first year.

The Tractive is the cheaper way to find out whether you actually use a tracker, because the entry cost is low and it bills monthly if you want. The Fi asks for more up front and an annual commitment, which is the right trade only if you already know you'll keep it running.

The practical advice: if you have never owned a dog tracker, the risk isn't buying the wrong one, it's discovering you don't use it. Start with the one that costs least to abandon.

Common questions

Does either one work without a subscription?
No. Both are cellular devices and both stop reporting position if the plan lapses. If you want a tracker with no recurring cost, you're looking at a Bluetooth tag instead, which is a different tool with a much shorter range.

Which has better range?
Neither has "range" in the way people mean it. Both report their position over the mobile network, so the limit is cellular coverage, not distance from you. Tractive's multi-carrier support is the more forgiving of the two in mixed coverage.

Can I put either on a cat or a small dog?
The Tractive XL is built for large dogs and is heavy for a small one; Tractive sells smaller variants. The Fi replaces a collar buckle and needs a collar sized for it.

Is the Fi worth roughly double the hardware cost?
Only if you'll keep it running for years. Over a single season the subscription dominates the total either way, and the Tractive is the cheaper experiment.

Our pick

For a dog that's off-leash regularly, the Fi Series 3+ is the one we'd choose, almost entirely because a tracker you don't have to think about is a tracker that's charged when it matters. For a first tracker, a large dog, or a household that wants to test the idea before committing, the Tractive GPS Dog XL is the sensible starting point.
