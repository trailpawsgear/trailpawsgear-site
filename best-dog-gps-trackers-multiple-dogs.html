Every GPS dog tracker review on the internet assumes you own one dog. Owners with two, three, or a whole pack hit a problem those reviews never mention: most trackers bill per collar, not per household, so the "affordable" subscription you saw advertised quietly doubles or triples the moment you add a second dog. We looked at four systems that handle multiple dogs differently — one that discounts additional collars outright, two that let you track the whole pack from a single app but still charge per tracker, and one no-frills budget option for a second dog that doesn't need the full feature set.

None of us tested these side by side with a multi-dog household over a full season. What follows is built from manufacturer specifications, support documentation, and retailer and owner reporting on pricing and real-world battery life, compared directly against each other — not first-hand long-term trail testing.

The math nobody mentions: subscriptions don't share

Tractive's own support documentation is explicit about this: each tracker needs its own subscription plan, because each one carries its own cellular connection and data fees, the same way a second phone line does. You can view and track every dog on one account in the app, but the bill multiplies per collar. Fi works the same way — there's no bundled rate for a second or third collar on file. SpotOn is the one system here that builds a per-dog discount directly into checkout: it knocks $100 off automatically for each additional collar you buy, and support will quote volume pricing for four or more dogs. None of the four advertises a true "family plan" that covers multiple pets under one flat subscription.

The short version

If you've got a yard with two or more dogs and want them contained without a buried wire, SpotOn is the only system here that actually prices in a discount for the second collar, even though the sticker price is steep. For tracking multiple dogs on trail from one app, Tractive's long-battery XL edition is the easiest to live with day to day — you'll just pay full subscription price per dog, same as everywhere else. Fi Series 3 is worth the look if you care more about escape alerts and activity data than live trail tracking, and its three-month battery is the best of the bunch for owners juggling multiple chargers. Whistle Switch is the one to grab if you already have a system you like and just need a cheap second unit, not a second full-featured tracker.

Specifications, pricing, and subscription details are based on manufacturer listings, support documentation, and retailer data at time of writing and may change — multi-pet discounts in particular are easy for a brand to add or drop. This guide reflects research and side-by-side comparison, not first-hand long-term testing with a multi-dog household — we'll update it as we get trail time with each system.
