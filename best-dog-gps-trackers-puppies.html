The Best GPS Trackers for Puppies in 2026

A puppy GPS tracker has to solve a problem adult-dog trackers don't: the dog wearing it is still growing, often too small for standard hardware, and usually not yet reliable on recall. That rules out a lot of trackers outright — some brands state a minimum weight before the device is even considered safe or useful to attach. We compared four trackers that are either built specifically for small, growing dogs or commonly recommended for puppies, on device weight, minimum size guidance, battery life, and whether a subscription is required to make the thing work at all.

One thing to flag before the comparison: none of these are collars in the harness sense. Three are small GPS or Bluetooth modules that attach to a collar or harness strap your puppy already wears; Fi replaces the buckle itself. None of them are a substitute for puppy-proofing your yard or for recall training — a tracker tells you where your puppy went, not how to stop them from going.

Check the minimum size before you buy

The single most common mistake with a puppy tracker isn't picking the wrong brand — it's buying hardware sized for an adult dog. A device that's harmless on a 40-pound Lab can be genuinely uncomfortable, or simply too heavy proportionally, on an 8-week-old puppy. Where a brand publishes a minimum weight or size, as Tractive does at roughly 9 lb, treat it as a hard floor rather than a suggestion, and size up to a lighter option like Jiobit or an AirTag in the meantime if your puppy doesn't clear it yet.

The short version

For most owners planning to keep the same tracker brand from puppyhood into adulthood, the Fi Series 3 in its Extra Small band is the best overall pick — it's built to size up as the dog grows, and the GPS-plus-LTE connection doesn't depend on a nearby phone. For the smallest toy-breed puppies, where every gram matters, the 18-gram Jiobit is the safer fit. Once a puppy clears roughly 9 pounds and starts testing the yard fence, the Tractive GPS DOG 6's longer battery life and virtual fence alerts earn their subscription cost. And for owners who don't want another monthly bill and mostly stick to walks near home, an Apple AirTag with a proper collar holder is the only option here with no subscription — in exchange for real GPS coverage.
