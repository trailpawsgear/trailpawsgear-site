A GPS collar that stops working the first time your dog wades into a creek isn't a safety device — it's a paperweight with a subscription. Water crossings, sudden downpours, and mud are routine on trail, so we looked specifically at how four popular GPS dog trackers handle actual water exposure: what IP rating they carry (if any), how deep and how long they're rated to survive, and what happens to the signal once a dog is submerged.

One thing to know going in: no GPS tracker transmits underwater. Every device here shows your dog's last known position before they went in and reacquires a signal once they surface — the waterproof rating protects the hardware, not the connection.

The short version

For a dog that swims regularly or hikes near water, the Fi Series 3+ has the deepest published water rating and the longest battery life of any wearable tracker here, making it the easiest to trust and forget about. If cost matters more than swim depth, the Tractive GPS Dog XL covers rain, mud, and short dunks at a much lower price. Hunters and backcountry owners working outside cell coverage should look at the Garmin Alpha 200i despite the price, since it's the only tracker here that doesn't depend on LTE. And if you want health tracking bundled with location and can live without a hard water-resistance number, the Whistle Go Explore's battery life is hard to beat.

Specifications, pricing, and waterproof ratings are based on manufacturer listings, help-center documentation, and third-party testing at time of writing, and may change.
