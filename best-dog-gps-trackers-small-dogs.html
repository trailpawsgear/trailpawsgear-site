The Best GPS Trackers for Small Dogs in 2026

Most GPS dog tracker reviews are written around a 60-pound retriever. That's a problem if your dog is a 7-pound Chihuahua or a 12-pound terrier, because the two things that make a tracker work for a big dog — a bulky case and a battery that needs frequent charging anyway — are exactly what makes a tracker useless or unsafe on a small one. A unit that's fine clipped to a Labrador's collar can outweigh a toy breed's collar hardware, chafe at the neck, or simply not fit a collar under an inch wide.

We compared five trackers that are explicitly built, or clearly suitable, for dogs under roughly 20 pounds: one made specifically for toy breeds and cats, one that borrows a child-tracker platform, one general-purpose tracker with a genuinely small model, an ultra-light 4G tag aimed mostly at cats but sized for tiny dogs too, and a "mini" tracker that turned out, once we checked the actual spec sheet, to still be the heaviest thing on this list. We looked at weight, battery life, waterproofing, and whether a subscription is required — because for a toy breed, grams and days matter more than range.

The short version

For most toy and small breeds, the Fi Mini is the easiest recommendation — it's the lightest purpose-built dog tracker here, fits narrow collars, and bundles the first year of service into the purchase. If your dog gets lost indoors as often as outdoors, the Jiobit's combination of GPS, cellular, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth is worth the higher subscription cost. Dogs around 9 lb and up who need to go multiple days between charges do best on the Tractive DOG 6. The Pawfit Lite is the cheapest way in if your dog is genuinely tiny and your outings are short. And despite its name, the Dogtra Pathfinder 2 Mini isn't really a small-dog tracker at all — it belongs on this list only as the no-subscription option for dogs at the upper end of "small."

Specifications above come from manufacturer spec sheets and retailer listings at time of writing and may change — we flagged the one case (Dogtra) where the brand's own materials disagreed with each other. This guide reflects research and side-by-side spec comparison, not first-hand long-term trail testing with each tracker on a small dog; we'll update it as we get hands-on time with these units.
